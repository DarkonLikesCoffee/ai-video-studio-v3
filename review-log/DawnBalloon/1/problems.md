# DawnBalloon — review round 1

## Ranked aesthetic problems

1. **Balloon rig readability (worst):** The envelope reads as a simple stacked oval, while the basket and four ropes are tiny, clipped, or almost invisible in the sampled views. The payload should read immediately as a hot-air balloon, especially during the middle tracking shot. Enlarge the basket/rope silhouette and keep it inside the lower envelope frame.
2. **Shot 1 does not communicate a valley-floor lift-off:** The balloon is already floating above the ground at frame 0, and the low camera composition puts it small against oversized angular peaks. It rises out of frame by frame 180 instead of visibly passing the camera with a clear ground-to-sky trajectory. Start the balloon lower and reframe the camera to follow the climb without clipping.
3. **Shot 3 loses the sunrise subject:** Frames 420–570 are pale and low-contrast; the balloon and basket are barely distinguishable through the cloud/fog layers. Reduce cloud opacity and fog density, warm the horizon, and preserve a readable silhouette against the sunlit sky.

## Motion check

- **Subject direction:** The envelope rises (+Y) and drifts forward (+X) in the implementation. That direction remains consistent across the sampled sequence.
- **Actual sampled result:** Motion is visible from frame 0 to 120 and continues through the middle. However, shot 1 clips the balloon by frame 180; later shots keep a small balloon while mountain/background displacement is subtle. Frames 420, 480, 540, and 570 show little obvious parallax at overview scale.
- **Verdict:** Motion exists, but the camera framing and low-contrast shot 3 weaken the motion read. Round 2 must preserve the envelope, basket, and ropes in frame while making cloud/mountain layers slide at different rates.
